# Google's policy and enforcement on incentivized reviews (state of play, November 2026)

Scope: Google Maps / Google Business Profile (GBP) rules on reviews given in exchange for a reward or a chance to win a prize, how Google enforces them, which ways of asking for a review are allowed, and the regulation that applies on top (US FTC, UK DMCC/CMA, EU Omnibus/UCPD, France). Research date: 2026-11-11. Labels used below: **[OFFICIAL]** = Google policy text or a regulator's text; **[REGULATOR]** = a regulator's position; **[PRACTITIONER]** = local-SEO practitioners or press (anecdotal); **[LAWYER]** = commentary by legal practitioners.

## 1. Exact current wording of Google's policies (Maps UGC policy and GBP review guidelines)

### Takeaway
Google's current Maps contribution policy bans "incentives – such as payment, discounts, free goods and/or services" in exchange for reviews. It removes "content that has been posted due to an incentive offered by a business", and it forbids businesses to "selectively solicit positive reviews". The GBP help page "Tips to get more reviews" says incentives are "strictly prohibited". Neither page uses the words "prize", "contest" or "chance to win" (see section 2).

### Cited Findings
- **[OFFICIAL]** The page is titled "Prohibited & restricted content – Maps User Generated Content Policy Help". It has no visible last-updated date, only "©2026 Google". — [Google Maps UGC policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114)
- **[OFFICIAL] Fake engagement:** "Fake engagement is not allowed and will be removed." The examples include "Reviews or ratings that have been paid for, directly or in kind." Merchants must not offer "incentives – such as payment, discounts, free goods and/or services" in exchange for reviews. — [Google Maps UGC policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114)
- **[OFFICIAL] Rating manipulation:** "Rating Manipulation includes Incentivized or Biased Reviews, which are not allowed and will be removed from Maps." The examples include "Content that has been posted due to an incentive offered by a business" and "Content posted following requests for revision or removal of a review or rating in exchange for an incentive." — [Google Maps UGC policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114)
- **[OFFICIAL] Conflict of interest:** "Content that is based on a conflict of interest", including "current or former employment, a contractual or consultory relationship". — [Google Maps UGC policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114)
- **[OFFICIAL] Solicitation rules:** Merchants must not "Discourage or prohibit negative reviews, or selectively solicit positive reviews from customers". Also: "When soliciting reviews, merchants should not require or pressure users to leave ratings." — [Google Maps UGC policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114)
- **[OFFICIAL] GBP "Tips to get more reviews":** "To leave reviews, you can ask customers to visit a Google link or scan a QR code." The page says it is prohibited to offer "incentives, like free or discounted goods or services, in exchange for customers to post reviews", or to "change reviews, or remove negative reviews", and calls this "strictly prohibited". — [GBP Help: Tips to get more reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Google's April 2025 blog post states: "buying fake five-star reviews from people who never visited a business is strictly prohibited". — [Google blog, 7 Apr 2025](https://blog.google/products/maps/google-business-profiles-ai-fake-reviews/)

### Inferences
- The policy is written in terms of an *incentive offered by a business* in exchange for posting. It is not limited to paying for fake or positive reviews. A real customer's honest review still breaks the rule if an incentive led to it.

### Gaps
- No last-modified date is shown on either Google page, so I cannot date the current wording.
- I did not fetch the French-language version of the policy, which may be the version a Moroccan merchant sees. Its wording may differ slightly.

## 2. Does the ban cover a mere chance to win, rewards regardless of sentiment, or "feedback" with the Google review optional?

### Takeaway
On rewards regardless of sentiment, the policy is clear: any incentive tied to posting a review is banned, positive or negative. On chance-to-win, Google's policy text is silent. The only explicit statement is a Google spokesperson's from about 2018 ("contests to generate reviews are a violation of policy"), and practitioners consistently treat a lottery, wheel or draw as an incentive. On optional reviews, no official Google text says that a reward for general "feedback", with the Google review optional, is allowed. Google's in-app question to users ("Does this business offer rewards in exchange for reviews?") would likely flag such schemes either way.

### Cited Findings
- **[OFFICIAL]** The ban targets "Content that has been posted due to an incentive offered by a business". The policy contains no exception for unconditional or sentiment-neutral incentives. — [Google Maps UGC policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** PinMeTo, reading the GBP Fake Engagement policy, says any incentive tied to leaving a review is prohibited "whether the review is positive or negative". — [PinMeTo](https://www.pinmeto.com/news/google-incentivized-reviews-penalty-2026/)
- **[OFFICIAL, older: c. 2018]** Search Engine Land reported a law firm that ran a contest to get Google reviews. A Google spokesperson said "contests to generate reviews are a violation of policy", one review disappeared, and the firm was penalized. — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/law-firm-used-contest-solicit-google-reviews-sees-one-disappear-292721)
- **[OFFICIAL, older: 2013]** A Google response quoted by Mike Blumenthal: "any incentive offered in return for a review of a specific business is against our policy". Blumenthal also concluded that review contests which donate to charity violate the guidelines. — [Blumenthals 2013-01-14](https://blumenthals.com/blog/2013/01/14/google-review-contests-violate-guidelines/), [Blumenthals 2013-01-18](https://blumenthals.com/blog/2013/01/18/do-review-contests-that-donate-to-charity-violate-googles-review-guidelines-yes/)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** BrightLocal: a contest open to every reviewer, not only positive ones, still violates Google's terms. — [BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/blog/risky-review-schemes-that-could-sink-your-business/)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** In a Local Search Forum thread, practitioners say Google "purposely leaves things vague" but that a contest or chance to win is still treated as an incentive. — [Local Search Forum](https://localsearchforum.com/threads/online-review-incentives-smart-marketing-or-asking-for-trouble.51209/latest)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Since about September 2025, Google Maps has asked users at a business: "Does this business offer rewards in exchange for reviews?" (Yes / No / Not sure). Colan Nielsen of Sterling Sky reports that reviews get deleted when users answer "Yes". — [PPC Land, 5 Jun 2026](https://ppc.land/google-maps-is-now-asking-users-if-businesses-paid-for-their-reviews/)
- **Contrast with regulators.** UK CMA guidance says a prize draw is "unlikely to amount to commissioning" under the UK banned practice. The US FTC rule allows unconditional incentives (see section 6). **Google's policy is stricter than both laws.** — [CMA208 fn. 8](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67eeb64fe9c76fa33048c790/CMA208_-_Fake_reviews_guidance.pdf), [FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)

### Inferences
- A lucky wheel or scratch card unlocked by leaving a Google review is a reward offered by the business in exchange for posting. It falls within "content that has been posted due to an incentive offered by a business", whatever the odds and whatever the star rating.
- The "feedback with optional Google review" variant has two problems. If the reward is in practice linked to the Google review (shown after it, or more likely after it), it is still an incentive. And if only happy feedback-givers are sent to Google, it also becomes review gating (section 3). A reward for internal feedback only, with no Google link anywhere in the reward flow, is the only variant I could not find any Google text against. That conclusion is mine, not Google's.

### Gaps
- No current (2024–2026) official Google statement uses the words "lottery", "sweepstakes", "contest", "wheel" or "chance to win". The explicit Google statements on contests date from 2013 and about 2018.
- No official Google text on rewarding internal feedback with the public review optional.

## 3. Review gating (sending only happy customers to Google)

### Takeaway
Review gating is explicitly prohibited by Google ("selectively solicit positive reviews from customers"). The UK CMA also names "encouraging just those who are satisfied to leave reviews" as cherry-picking that may infringe the law. The FTC says the practice "could violate the FTC Act" even though its 2024 rule has no specific ban.

### Cited Findings
- **[OFFICIAL]** Merchants must not "Discourage or prohibit negative reviews, or selectively solicit positive reviews from customers." — [Google Maps UGC policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114)
- **[REGULATOR, UK]** CMA208 para 4.5: "Cherry picking positive reviews for publication over negative ones might be done either through suppressing negative reviews that have been submitted or by encouraging just those who are satisfied to leave reviews." Para 4.4(a)(iii) says traders should not interfere "by arbitrarily stopping and starting review invitations". — [CMA208, 4 Apr 2025](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67eeb64fe9c76fa33048c790/CMA208_-_Fake_reviews_guidance.pdf)
- **[REGULATOR, US]** On asking only happy customers: "The rule does not contain a specific prohibition against such conduct. But this practice could violate the FTC Act." — [FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)
- **[REGULATOR, US]** The FTC gives "Tell your friends about all the fun you had at Jane's Arcade for a chance to win prizes" as a prohibited prompt, because it implies the review must be positive. — [FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)

### Inferences
- A typical "rate us 1–5 inside the app; 4–5 goes to Google, 1–3 goes to private feedback" flow is review gating under Google's wording.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve a dated Google announcement of when "selectively solicit" was added. It is widely reported as 2018, but that date is unverified in this research.

## 4. Enforcement: penalties, restrictions, warning banners, countries, documented cases

### Takeaway
Google removes violating reviews. Under the Fake Engagement policy it can also block new reviews for a set period, unpublish existing reviews, and display a public warning that fake reviews were removed. The owner is emailed first and can appeal. In practice, a first violation has typically meant a review block of about one month and a repeat violation about two months (July 2026 report). The warnings started in the UK (CMA undertakings, Jan 2025), launched in the US, UK and India by April 2025, and were announced to expand globally. Google has also been deleting reviews in bulk where Maps users report rewards.

### Cited Findings
- **[OFFICIAL]** "Business Profile restrictions for policy violations" lists possible restrictions, "not limited to" these: "will not be able to receive new reviews or ratings for set period of time"; "existing reviews or ratings will be unpublished for set period of time"; "will display a warning to let consumers know that fake reviews were removed". They apply when "a business owner has violated our Fake Engagement policy", in addition to removing the violating reviews. "We will notify business owners via email if we plan to apply a restriction". Appeals go through a re-review form. The page gives no durations and no country list. — [GBP Help 14114287](https://support.google.com/business/answer/14114287?hl=en)
- **[PRACTITIONER] Durations and escalation:** In early July 2026, Ben Fisher shared a restriction notice in which Google found additional incentivised reviews (a repeat violation). The notice read: "Customers won't be able to post new ratings and reviews or see past ratings and reviews for 2 months", alongside a public warning. Fisher said one month had been the standard first-violation penalty. PinMeTo says penalties are additive across violations, which can include ones resolved in a previous quarter or ones at different locations of the same brand. — [PinMeTo](https://www.pinmeto.com/news/google-review-restrictions-stack-repeat-violations-2026/)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Other reported durations vary; one consultant cites blocks of 6–8 months. Treat all durations as anecdotal, since Google's own text only says "set period". — [WebSearch summary of Sterling Sky "review jail" and others](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/review-jail-for-fake-5-star-google-reviews/)
- **[REGULATOR/OFFICIAL, UK, 24 Jan 2025]** Under CMA undertakings, Google committed to the following for UK businesses that boost ratings with fake reviews:
  - deactivating their ability to receive reviews;
  - for repeat offenders, deleting all reviews for 6 months or more;
  - adding "prominent 'warning' alerts" to their profiles;
  - banning UK and foreign individuals who repeatedly post fake reviews of UK businesses;
  - providing a reporting tool for "incentives" (payment or reward for a positive review);
  - reporting to the CMA for 3 years.

  Google planned to extend the sanctions to non-UK businesses, including EU businesses, by the end of 2025. The undertakings cover business and service reviews on Search and Maps, not product reviews. — [CMA press release](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/cma-secures-important-changes-from-google-to-tackle-fake-reviews)
- **[OFFICIAL, 7 Apr 2025]** Alerts that a place recently had suspicious five-star reviews removed launched in the US, UK and India, and were to "expand globally starting next month". Figures for 2024: more than 240M policy-violating reviews blocked or removed, more than 70M edits blocked, more than 12M fake profiles removed, and posting restrictions on more than 900k accounts. — [Google blog](https://blog.google/products/maps/google-business-profiles-ai-fake-reviews/)
- **[OFFICIAL, 16 Apr 2026]** If Google sees "a sudden spike in spam reviews" (in context, review-bombing and extortion), it removes the content, pauses new reviews, alerts the owner, and shows "a notification banner to let consumers know why contributions are temporarily paused". The protections are "rolling out globally in the coming weeks". — [Google blog: New ways we're protecting businesses on Maps](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/maps/new-ways-were-protecting-businesses-on-maps/)
- **[PRACTITIONER, secondary]** Reported 2025 Trust & Safety figures: more than 292M policy-violating reviews blocked or removed, more than 1B published, 79M edits blocked, posting restrictions on more than 782k accounts, and more than 13M fake profiles removed. I could not open the primary Google report, so these are secondary reports. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-maps-uses-gemini-to-stop-fake-edits-and-blocks-review-extortion-scams/), [Adapt Digital](https://adaptdigitalsolutions.com/articles/google-review-policy-2026-compliance-guide/)
- **[PRACTITIONER] Bulk deletions linked to the "rewards" question:** Claudia Tomina's client data shows 496 review deletions in November 2025 and 1,045 in December 2025. Many of the deleted reviews were posted in August–September 2024, so enforcement reaches back to older reviews. Mike Blumenthal sees the deletions as a deliberate Google decision. — [PPC Land, 5 Jun 2026](https://ppc.land/google-maps-is-now-asking-users-if-businesses-paid-for-their-reviews/)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Serious cases can lead to suspension of the listing. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-maps-is-now-asking-users-if-businesses-paid-for-their-reviews/)
- **Documented wheel cases:** I found no documented, sourced case of a business penalised specifically for a wheel-of-fortune or "avis contre cadeau" scheme. The closest documented case is the contest case above. Wheel-for-review tools are openly marketed in France: RushUp offers a QR-code wheel game for restaurants that then invites the customer to leave a Google review, with the gift redeemed on a later visit. Up Review and Custplace offer similar tools. — [La Fabrique du Net: RushUp](https://www.lafabriquedunet.fr/logiciel/rushup), [Product Hunt: Up Review](https://www.producthunt.com/p/up-review/up-review-2), [Custplace](https://fr.custplace.com/business/?p=6700)

### Inferences
- For a JitPlus merchant, the realistic downside of a wheel-for-review scheme is:
  - **Detected incentivized reviews:** removal, which can be retroactive.
  - **First violation:** a block of about one month on new reviews and hiding of existing reviews, plus a public warning banner.
  - **Repeat violation:** a longer block.
  - **Serious cases:** possibly suspension of the listing.

  Because penalties are reported to stack across a brand's locations, a platform-wide feature could expose every participating merchant at once.

### Gaps
- No official, current country list for the consumer warning banner. The confirmed rollouts are UK (2024–25), then US, UK and India (Apr 2025), with "global" expansion announced. I could not confirm whether it is live in Morocco.
- No Google statement confirms whether reviews flagged through the "rewards" question are deleted automatically. That claim comes from practitioners.

## 5. How Google detects incentivized or fake reviews

### Takeaway
Google publicly describes only general mechanisms: machine learning plus human analysts, monitoring for "unusual patterns" and spikes, re-checking reviews months after posting, Gemini-assisted models for edits, user and owner reports, and, since 2025, an in-app question asking users whether the business offers rewards. Google does not officially document specific signals such as same wifi, same device or QR-code patterns.

### Cited Findings
- **[OFFICIAL, Nov 2023]** Google's systems "are constantly monitoring for unusual patterns in contributed content". Example given: a sudden spike of 1-star reviews on a Missouri bar led to ratings being disabled temporarily, removals, and an investigation of the accounts involved. — [Google blog: How Google Maps protects against fake content](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/maps/how-google-maps-protects-against-fake-content/)
- **[OFFICIAL, Apr 2025]** Google revisits reviews more often, including months after posting, to catch new abuse patterns. It uses a Gemini-assisted model to flag suspicious profile edits. — [Google blog](https://blog.google/products/maps/google-business-profiles-ai-fake-reviews/)
- **[OFFICIAL, Apr 2026]** Google detects "a sudden spike in spam reviews" and extortion attempts. — [Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/maps/new-ways-were-protecting-businesses-on-maps/)
- **[OFFICIAL/REGULATOR]** Consumer reporting of "incentives" is part of the CMA undertakings. — [CMA](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/cma-secures-important-changes-from-google-to-tackle-fake-reviews)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Google asks users in Maps "Does this business offer rewards in exchange for reviews?". Answers of "Yes" are reported to lead to deletions. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-maps-is-now-asking-users-if-businesses-paid-for-their-reviews/), [Thrive Agency](https://thriveagency.com/news/google-is-asking-users-if-businesses-offer-rewards-for-reviews/)
- **[REGULATOR, France]** The DGCCRF uses a tool called "Polygraphe" to spot businesses whose reviews look suspicious. — [Next.ink](https://next.ink/brief-article/la-dgccrf-traque-les-faux-avis-sur-internet-avec-son-polygraphe/)

### Inferences
- An in-store scheme generates reviews in bursts, from the same location, often on shop wifi, timed with redemptions and with similar short texts. Those match the "unusual patterns" Google says it monitors. Customers who won a gift will also answer "Yes" truthfully when Maps asks whether the business offers rewards.

### Gaps
- Google publishes no list of detection signals (IP, wifi, device, geolocation, QR-referral, text similarity). Any claim about specific signals would be speculation.

## 6. What is explicitly allowed

### Takeaway
Google explicitly allows and encourages:
- asking customers for reviews;
- sharing Google's official review link or QR code, generated from the Business Profile ("Read reviews → Get more reviews → Share");
- putting the link in thank-you emails, at the end of chats and on receipts;
- replying to reviews.

Asking must be done without pressure and without picking only happy customers.

### Cited Findings
- **[OFFICIAL]** "To leave reviews, you can ask customers to visit a Google link or scan a QR code." — [GBP Help: Tips to get more reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)
- **[OFFICIAL]** "Create a Google link or QR code to request reviews". In the Business Profile, go to Read Reviews → Get more reviews → Share icon, then copy the link or save the QR code image. Google suggests including it "in thank you emails", "at the end of a chat interaction", and "on your receipts". — [GBP Help 16816815](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16816815?hl=en), [Search Engine Land on QR codes](https://searchengineland.com/google-offers-qr-codes-to-get-reviews-from-customers-452966), [SERoundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-help-page-request-reviews-40677.html)
- **[OFFICIAL]** The limits on asking: no requiring or pressuring users to leave ratings, and no selective solicitation. — [Google Maps UGC policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Owners manage and reply to reviews from the Business Profile. — [GBP Help: Manage customer reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474050?hl=en)
- **[REGULATOR, UK]** Example of a lawful approach in CMA208: a service integrated with a client's order system that sends emails to *all* customers inviting them to leave a review. — [CMA208](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67eeb64fe9c76fa33048c790/CMA208_-_Fake_reviews_guidance.pdf)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Third-party guides note that some generated links open the Maps listing rather than the review form directly. They advise testing on a real phone before printing. — [Wiremo](https://wiremo.co/blog/how-to-get-your-google-review-link/)

### Inferences
- NFC cards or stands that point to the same official review link are just another way to carry the link. Post-purchase reminders sent to all customers are consistent with Google's own suggestions (receipts, thank-you emails). Neither involves an incentive.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the `g.page/r/...` or `search.google.com/local/writereview?placeid=...` URL formats from an official Google page. They are widely used by practitioners and should be tested directly.
- No official Google text mentions NFC.

## 7. Regulation on top of Google (US, UK, EU, France)

### Takeaway
Three legal regimes allow some forms of incentivized reviews that Google forbids:
- **US FTC:** unconditional incentives are lawful; incentives conditioned on sentiment are banned.
- **UK DMCC:** concealed incentivised reviews are a banned practice, but a prize draw is "unlikely to amount to commissioning". Cherry-picking may infringe the law, and facilitators are liable.
- **EU Omnibus / France:** fake or misrepresented reviews are blacklisted. Rewarded reviews are tolerated only if clearly disclosed.

None of this makes the scheme acceptable to Google, whose policy is the binding constraint. A platform that facilitates such schemes also carries its own legal exposure in the UK, as a facilitator, and in the US, as an intermediary.

### Cited Findings
- **US: FTC final rule, 16 CFR Part 465**
  - **[REGULATOR]** "The rule does not prohibit giving incentives for reviews, as long as there isn't an express or implied requirement" that the review express a particular sentiment. — [FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)
  - **[REGULATOR]** Not disclosing incentives may still violate the FTC Act. — [FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)
  - **[REGULATOR]** A "chance to win prizes" prompt that implies a positive review is a prohibited example. — [FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)
  - **[REGULATOR]** Agencies and review brokers "are not immune from liability under the rule". — [FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)
  - **[REGULATOR]** Courts may impose civil penalties for knowing violations. — [FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)
  - **[LAWYER]** Penalty figures vary by source: $51,744 per violation (2024), and an inflation-adjusted $53,088 cited in 2026. Each day of an ongoing violation can count separately. — [BCLP](https://bclplaw.com/en-US/events-insights-news/part-6-what-the-ftcs-final-rule-on-reviews-and-testimonials-means-for-enforcement-penalties.html), [Benesch](https://www.beneschlaw.com/insight/five-stars-zero-tolerance-ftc-turns-up-enforcement-under-consumer-review-rule/)
  - **[LAWYER]** The FTC sent formal warning letters to nearly a dozen companies under Part 465 at the end of 2025. — [Benesch](https://www.beneschlaw.com/insight/five-stars-zero-tolerance-ftc-turns-up-enforcement-under-consumer-review-rule/)
- **UK: DMCC Act 2024, Sch. 20 para 13, and CMA208 (4 Apr 2025; in force 6 Apr 2025)**
  - **[REGULATOR]** A review "conceals the fact it has been incentivised" if the person was commissioned and that is "not made apparent". Commissioning examples: money, commissions, discounts or vouchers, freebies, free stays, invitations to events (para 2.9–2.10). — [CMA208](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67eeb64fe9c76fa33048c790/CMA208_-_Fake_reviews_guidance.pdf)
  - **[REGULATOR]** Footnote 8: "offering the chance to earn a reward which does not guarantee a direct benefit for the reviewer (for example ... entered into a prize draw) is unlikely to amount to commissioning in the context of the banned practice." — [CMA208](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67eeb64fe9c76fa33048c790/CMA208_-_Fake_reviews_guidance.pdf)
  - **[REGULATOR]** Cherry-picking: para 4.5 (see section 3). Para 4.7: publishers of incentivised reviews must distinguish them from other reviews. — [CMA208](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67eeb64fe9c76fa33048c790/CMA208_-_Fake_reviews_guidance.pdf)
  - **[REGULATOR]** Para 7.1: it is "prohibited to offer services to traders for the facilitating of the submission, or commissioning of banned reviews". — [CMA208](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67eeb64fe9c76fa33048c790/CMA208_-_Fake_reviews_guidance.pdf)
  - **[REGULATOR]** The CMA can fine up to 10% of global turnover. — [CMA press release](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/cma-secures-important-changes-from-google-to-tackle-fake-reviews), [CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/no-more-faux-five-stars-the-dmcc-act-bans-fake-reviews)
  - **[LAWYER]** The guidance was later consolidated, with an update noted in October 2025. — [WebSearch summary; Linklaters](https://www.linklaters.com/en/insights/blogs/linkingcompetition/2025/may/consumer-deep-dive_fake-reviews)
- **EU: Omnibus Directive 2019/2161, amending UCPD Annex I**
  - **[LAWYER]** Point 23b bans claiming that reviews come from real users or buyers without reasonable and proportionate checks. Point 23c bans submitting or commissioning false reviews or endorsements, or misrepresenting reviews, to promote products. — [Makeinfluence](https://www.makeinfluence.com/en/academy/fake-and-manipulated-reviews-what-the-eu-omnibus-directive-bans), [Couto, IFF](https://iff-hamburg.de/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Rute-Couto_Unfair-Commercial-Practices-in-the-Digital-Environment.pdf)
  - **[LAWYER]** Traders that give access to reviews must say how they ensure the reviews come from real users. — [Council doc](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-11352-2024-REV-1/en/pdf)
  - **[LAWYER]** According to French lawyers, the Omnibus framework allows rewarded reviews if the reward is clearly disclosed. — [Blog du Modérateur, 17 Jul 2025](https://www.blogdumoderateur.com/offrir-contrepartie-contre-avis-legal-ou-pas/)
- **France: DGCCRF / Code de la consommation**
  - **[LAWYER]** Offering a reward for a positive Google review can be "une pratique commerciale pouvant être qualifiée de trompeuse" (Jérôme Giusti). Penalties cited: "deux ans d'emprisonnement et/ou 300 000 € d'amende", up to 10% of average annual turnover or 50% of the cost of the practice, plus civil liability (Yann-Maël Larher). — [Blog du Modérateur](https://www.blogdumoderateur.com/offrir-contrepartie-contre-avis-legal-ou-pas/)

### Inferences
- A JitPlus feature that hands out wheel or scratch-card plays for Google reviews has two layers of exposure:
  - **Google, for the merchant:** it violates Google's policy everywhere, including Morocco, and could trigger the restrictions in section 4.
  - **Law, for JitPlus:** where UK, EU or French law applies, JitPlus could be treated as a facilitator or intermediary. The risk is higher if the flow also gates by sentiment or hides the reward.
- The UK prize-draw footnote and the FTC's allowance for unconditional incentives do **not** override Google's contractual policy.
