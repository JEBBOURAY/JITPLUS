## Q1. Morocco: loi n° 31-08, promotional lotteries (loteries publicitaires), premium sales (ventes avec primes) and misleading advertising

### Takeaway
Loi 31-08 (Chapitre 9 du Titre IV, arts. 60–64) regulates *loteries publicitaires*:
- every draw needs its own rules document (*règlement particulier*);
- the rules and the ads must be filed with the "administration compétente";
- the ads must carry set wording and a prize inventory;
- the participation slip must be separate from any order form or receipt;
- the organiser must send an end-of-operation report listing the winners.

The penalty is a fine of 50,000 to 200,000 MAD (art. 185). The law itself never mentions a huissier or notaire, and it contains no express "no purchase" rule. However, commentary and the Code pénal context point to free entry being the safe structure. Applying Chapter 9 depended on implementing regulations. Décret 2-12-503 (2013) is reported to supply the model template, but commentary from 2016 to 2022 said the receiving administration had still not been clearly designated.

### Inferences
- A wheel or scratch card that a merchant offers to customers is a *loterie publicitaire* within art. 60, since the art. 60 definition covers « quelles que soient les modalités de tirage au sort ». Each merchant is the *organisateur* and *fournisseur*, so the duties in arts. 61–64 fall mainly on the merchant.
- JitPlus could still be exposed as an accomplice. Art. 175 makes complicity punishable for misleading advertising, and the Code pénal applies generally.
- To comply in-app, each campaign should generate a merchant-specific règlement and show:
  - a prize list with nature, exact number and commercial value, in value order;
  - the mandatory wording « le règlement… est adressé, à titre gratuit… » with an address;
  - a winners log the merchant can export for the art. 64 report.
- Art. 56 likely does not bite on a free draw. A prize "won" by chance is not a premium given "on purchase". But giving every purchaser a guaranteed free gift of a different product could fall under art. 56 unless the gift is low value. The value threshold is set by décret; it was not checked.
- Art. 60 requires a participation slip separate from receipts. An in-app entry screen separate from payment probably satisfies this, but nothing confirms it.

### Gaps
- No official text was read from sgg.gov.ma or the BO, and the translation used is unofficial. The authoritative text is the Arabic text in the BO.
- Not confirmed: whether the Chapter 9 implementing regulations are now fully in force, which administration receives filings in 2026, any online filing procedure, and the actual model template under décret 2-12-503.
- Not found: any amendment to loi 31-08 since 2011 affecting lotteries, or any reported prosecution under art. 185.
- No source found confirms a huissier requirement in Morocco. A deposit with a notaire or huissier appears to be voluntary practice.

## Q2. Morocco: general lottery prohibition (Code pénal), the State monopoly (Loterie Nationale, MDJS), and when a free promotional draw falls outside them

### Takeaway
The Code pénal (arts. 282–285) punishes unauthorised lotteries. Its definition (art. 284) does **not** require any payment by participants, so on its face even a free draw is a "loterie". The practical safe harbour is therefore loi 31-08, arts. 60–64: a *loterie publicitaire* run by a supplier and complying with that chapter. In practice, a free promotional draw sits outside the State gambling monopoly only by complying with loi 31-08, never through the Code pénal itself.

### Cited findings
- **[TEXT, secondary transcription] Code pénal arts. 282–285:**
  - **Art. 282:** imprisonment of three months to one year and a fine of 1,200 to 100,000 MAD for running a gambling house without authorisation.
  - **Art. 283:** extends those penalties to « auteurs, organisateurs, administrateurs, préposés ou agents de loteries non autorisées par l'autorité publique ».
  - **Art. 284:** « Sont réputées loteries toutes opérations proposées au public sous quelque dénomination que ce soit et destinées à faire naître l'espérance d'un gain qui serait acquis par la voie du sort. »
  - **Art. 285:** one to three months in prison and a fine of 200 to 1,000 MAD for those who peddle, distribute or advertise unauthorised lottery tickets.
  - Art. 284 mentions no stake or payment requirement.
  - Source: [Bladi.net transcription of the Code pénal](https://www.bladi.net/code-penal-marocain.html). The official consolidated PDF is at [adala.justice.gov.ma](https://adala.justice.gov.ma/api/uploads/2025/01/07/code%20penal-1736256380053.pdf), but it was not read.
- **[COMMENTARY]** The Code pénal creates the « délit de loterie » (arts. 282 ff.), and loi 31-08 then regulates advertising lotteries as a specific regime. — [Mossadek blog](http://frssiwa.blogspot.com/2017/10/le-cadre-juridique-des-loteries.html)
- **[COMMENTARY] Art. 282 history:** art. 282 was completed by dahir portant loi n° 1-77-58 du 19 septembre 1977, which repealed the dahir du 27 décembre 1937. An official said unauthorised gaming is punishable under arts. 282–285. — [le360](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/paris-sportifs-leconomie-marocaine-siphonnee-par-les-plateformes-en-ligne_MX2TMOOT4FBMLMKCIQPFD4CKBU/); listing in the [adala Code pénal PDF](https://adala.justice.gov.ma/api/uploads/2025/01/07/code%20penal-1736256380053.pdf)
- **[COMMENTARY] State monopoly:** the Loterie Nationale (1971) and the MDJS hold monopolies over games of chance, sports betting and lotteries. — [Hespress](https://fr.hespress.com/481668-paris-en-ligne-lessor-dun-marche-offshore-hors-controle.html); [La Vie Éco](https://www.lavieeco.com/?p=38422)
- **[COMMENTARY] Advertising ban:** loi 83-13 bans advertising games of chance in public and private audiovisual media. — [Medias24](https://medias24.com/2018/01/14/une-publicite-pour-le-loto-sur-2m-fait-polemique/); [Bladi](https://www.bladi.net/maroc-loto-television,42940.html)

### Inferences
- There are two risk factors: (a) a mechanic that requires payment or purchase looks more like gambling than promotion, and (b) a merchant that ignores arts. 60–64 loses the 31-08 framework and, in theory, could face art. 283. Free entry plus full 31-08 compliance is the defensible structure.
- A "review or follow to spin" mechanic is free in money terms. No source considers whether a non-monetary action, such as a review, follow or data, counts as "consideration".
- Prizes should not be cash or cash-equivalents, and the feature should not be marketed with gambling vocabulary ("pari", "mise", "casino"). This reduces confusion with MDJS games and with the loi 83-13 advertising ban. This is a prudential point, not sourced.

### Gaps
- Not confirmed: whether a merchant draw that complies with loi 31-08 is treated as « autorisée par l'autorité publique » for the purposes of art. 283, or whether a separate authorisation exists. Moroccan counsel must confirm this.
- No authoritative case law or ministry position was found on free in-store or app draws.

## Q3. Morocco: rules or regulator positions on fake or incentivised online reviews

### Takeaway
No Moroccan statute, decree or regulator guidance specifically on online reviews (fake or rewarded) was found. The applicable general rules are loi 31-08 art. 21 (misleading advertising), art. 23 (advertising must be identifiable as such) and Google's own policies. Google's policy is the binding constraint in practice, because it bans incentivised reviews outright.

### Cited findings
- **[TEXT]** Art. 21 (misleading advertising) and art. 23 (advertising nature, including « cadeaux… primes… loteries publicitaires », must be shown clearly), with penalties under arts. 174 and 176. See Q1 — [loi 31-08 (unofficial translation)](https://mahkamaty.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2013/11/%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%88%D9%86-31.08-%D9%84%D8%AD%D9%85%D8%A7%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B3%D8%AA%D9%87%D9%84%D9%83-%D9%81%D8%B1%D9%86%D8%B3%D9%8A1.pdf)
- **[COMMENTARY quoting platform policies]** Third-party platform policies:
  - Google forbids businesses to « proposer des avantages (comme un paiement, des remises, des produits et/ou services gratuits) en échange » of reviews.
  - TripAdvisor forbids rewards « sous aucun prétexte ».
  - Trustpilot forbids writing a review « si on vous a alloué ou proposé un incitatif ».
  - Source: [Blog du Modérateur, 17 Jul 2025](https://www.blogdumoderateur.com/offrir-contrepartie-contre-avis-legal-ou-pas/)

### Inferences
- Even where Moroccan law is silent, "Google review in exchange for a spin" breaches Google's Maps user-contributed content policy. The consequences are review removal, possible restrictions on the merchant's Business Profile, and a risk to JitPlus's own Google relationships. This is the main practical blocker in Morocco.
- If the incentive is not disclosed, the resulting reviews could be argued to be misleading advertising under art. 21. The merchant is the *annonceur* and responsible « à titre principal » (art. 175). No precedent was found.
- Rewarding social *follows* is not addressed by any Moroccan text found. Platform terms are the main constraint: Instagram, TikTok and Facebook generally prohibit "follow-for-reward" or inauthentic engagement. Their policy texts were not read this session.

### Gaps
- Nothing was found on mcinet.gov.ma or from Moroccan consumer associations on fake reviews.
- Unknown whether the 31-08 reform discussions (if any) in 2025–2026 address online reviews.
- The exact current text of Google's "incentivised reviews" policy was not fetched (it is cited via secondary source), and Meta's and TikTok's policies were not read.

## Q4. Morocco: loi 09-08 and CNDP rules on collecting a Google name, social handle or screenshot to verify the action

### Takeaway
Collecting a participant's Google display name, social handle or a screenshot is processing of personal data under loi 09-08. The main obligations:
- prior notification to the CNDP (déclaration), or authorisation (art. 12);
- information of participants;
- prior consent for any direct marketing (art. 10).

The CNDP has a standard declaration model for client prospecting and promotion (délibération 32-2015), a 2025 prospecting factsheet, and a December 2025 simplified-declaration model for cookies. No CNDP text specific to contests was found.

### Cited findings
- **[COMMENTARY/official PDFs]** CNDP délibération n° 32-2015 du 13/02/2015 is a model declaration that covers prospecting and promotion actions towards clients « dans le respect de l'article 10 de la loi 09-08 ». The CNDP warns against direct prospecting without prior consent (art. 10). Processing must be notified before it starts (art. 12). Failing to declare or seek authorisation is an offence under Chapter VII. — [CNDP délibération 32-2015](https://www.cndp.ma/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/deliberation-n-32-2015-13-02-2015.pdf); [CNDP fiche Prospection (Jul 2025)](https://www.cndp.ma/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/fiche_CNDP_Prospection_RT_fr.pdf); [CNDP communiqué](https://www.cndp.ma/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/CNDP-DEC-Communiqu_Version_franaise_20221220.pdf)
- **[COMMENTARY/official PDF]** Délibération D-939-2025 of 28/11/2025 is a simplified declaration model for cookies and terminal storage. It is relevant if the web or app flow sets trackers. — [CNDP](https://www.cndp.ma/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Deliberation-N%C2%B0-D-939-2025-du-28112025-relative-au-modele-de-declaration-simplifiee-relative-au-traitement-des-donnees-a-caractere-personnel-de-cookies-sur-un-terminal.pdf)
- **[COMMENTARY]** The CNDP identifies contests as a major source of illicit personal-data trading. — [La Vie Éco](https://www.lavieeco.com/affaires/jeux-concours-comment-la-loi-sur-le-consommateur-a-fait-le-menage/)
- **[UNCONFIRMED]** From background knowledge, loi 09-08 art. 53 sets a fine of 10,000 to 100,000 MAD for processing without declaration or authorisation, and art. 43 restricts transfers abroad (for example, a Google Cloud region outside Morocco) to countries with adequate protection or to CNDP-authorised transfers. Not checked this session.

### Inferences
- Roles: the merchant is likely the controller (*responsable du traitement*) for its campaign data, and JitPlus is a processor (*sous-traitant*) or a joint controller, depending on whether JitPlus reuses the data across merchants.
- JitPlus needs its own CNDP declaration (and possibly a transfer authorisation) for the platform. Its merchant terms should allocate CNDP duties.
- Data minimisation: verifying a review should ideally not require storing screenshots. Better options are a self-declaration, or a time-limited check deleted after the prize is awarded.
- Participation must not be conditional on accepting marketing (art. 10 consent must be separate and free).

### Gaps
- No CNDP deliberation specific to contests or *jeux-concours* was found.
- The exact current numbering and penalties in loi 09-08 were not verified.
- Not checked: any 2025–2026 replacement bill for loi 09-08.

## Q5. Morocco: cultural and religious sensitivity (qimar / maysir) and how brands structure *jeux-concours*

### Takeaway
No reliable source found this session addresses religious-law views on free promotional draws in Morocco. ## Q6. France: promotional lotteries (Code de la consommation L121-20, Code de la sécurité intérieure L320-1 ff.) and whether a deposit with a commissaire de justice is still required

### Takeaway
Since the loi Hamon (2014) and the loi of 20 December 2014, French promotional lotteries are lawful « dès lors qu'elles ne sont pas déloyales » (L121-20 C. conso). They fall outside the CSI ban on lotteries (L320-1 ff.), which requires a « sacrifice financier » among its conditions. Depositing the rules with a huissier (now a *commissaire de justice*) is **no longer compulsory**, but it remains recommended as evidence.

## Q7. France and EU: incentivised reviews (Omnibus 2019/2161, UCPD Annex I points 23b/23c, C. conso L121-4, L111-7-2, D111-16 ff.), legality if disclosed, and who is liable

### Takeaway
EU law, as amended by Omnibus (in force 28 May 2022), does not per se ban incentivised reviews. It blacklists:
- **23b:** claiming that reviews come from real buyers or users without reasonable checks;
- **23c:** submitting or commissioning false reviews, or misrepresenting reviews.

France additionally requires anyone collecting or publishing reviews to disclose whether a counterpart (*contrepartie*) was given (L111-7-2 + D111-17). Combined, a disclosed, content-neutral incentive may be lawful under consumer law. Two things are high-risk: an incentive tied to positive reviews, and an undisclosed incentive on a third-party platform. The incentive also breaches Google's terms, which forbid it regardless of disclosure.

### Cited findings
- **[COMMENTARY]** Omnibus added Annex I items 23b and 23c, applicable from 28 May 2022. The Commission's UCPD Guidance (2021/C 526/01) was adopted on 17 December 2021 and replaces the 2016 guidance. Items 23b and 23c are absolute bans that disclosure cannot cure for *false* reviews. — [Makeinfluence summary](https://www.makeinfluence.com/en/academy/fake-and-manipulated-reviews-what-the-eu-omnibus-directive-bans); [European Commission UCPD page](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/consumer-protection-law/unfair-commercial-practices-and-price-indication/unfair-commercial-practices-directive_fr)
- **[COMMENTARY] D111-17:** whoever manages online reviews must show near the reviews whether a control procedure exists, the publication date and ranking criteria. In a dedicated, easily accessible section, they must state « l'existence ou non de contrepartie fournie en échange du dépôt d'avis ». — [french-business-law.com, D111-17](https://french-business-law.com/french-legislation-art/article-d111-17-of-the-french-consumer-code); [MEDEF guide 2019](https://www.medef.com/uploads/media/node/0014/12/12016-guide-confiance-juin-2019-002-.pdf)
- **[COMMENTARY]** L111-7-2 (loi République numérique, 7 Oct 2016, specified by décret of 29 Sept 2017) imposes « une obligation de loyauté, de clarté et de transparence » on those who collect, moderate or publish reviews. A lawyer quoted says paying for positive reviews is forbidden and exposes the business to penal sanctions and civil liability. Avis Vérifiés accepts rewarded reviews labelled « Récompensé et vérifié ». — [Blog du Modérateur, 17 Jul 2025](https://www.blogdumoderateur.com/offrir-contrepartie-contre-avis-legal-ou-pas/)
- **[COMMENTARY] UK, for comparison:** CMA guidance (CMA208, under the DMCC Act 2024) treats concealed incentivised reviews as banned, and defines commissioning broadly to include discounts, vouchers and prize-draw entries. — [CMA208](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67eeb64fe9c76fa33048c790/CMA208_-_Fake_reviews_guidance.pdf)
- **[COMMENTARY]** BEUC (2025) advocates stronger rules on review reliability. — [BEUC X-2025-027](https://www.beuc.eu/sites/default/files/publications/BEUC-X-2025-027_how_to_make_online_reviews_more_reliable.pdf)
- **[UNCONFIRMED, from background knowledge of UCPD Guidance §4.2.5; EUR-Lex fetch failed]**
  - The Guidance says incentivised reviews (free product, discount, prize-draw entry) must be disclosed, failing which the practice can be a misleading omission (art. 7 UCPD).
  - Conditioning an incentive on a *positive* review, or filtering reviews to publish only positive ones ("review gating"), misrepresents reviews and can fall under 23c.
  - French transposition: L121-4 C. conso was amended by ordonnance 2021-1734 to add the corresponding blacklist items.
- **[COMMENTARY] Liability:** the trader (*annonceur*) is primarily liable. Intermediaries and agencies that "commission" fake reviews may be liable as authors or accomplices. — [Blog du Modérateur](https://www.blogdumoderateur.com/offrir-contrepartie-contre-avis-legal-ou-pas/); [CMA208](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67eeb64fe9c76fa33048c790/CMA208_-_Fake_reviews_guidance.pdf)

### Inferences
- **"Leave any review (positive or negative) → spin":** arguably lawful under EU/FR consumer law if disclosed. But the reviewer can't easily disclose on Google, and Google bans incentives entirely, so in practice it is non-compliant on Google Maps.
- **Spin only after a 4–5 star review:** high risk under 23c and L121-4, and a clear breach of Google policy.
- **Follow-for-spin:** EU consumer law is less directly engaged, because a follow is not a review. It still breaches most platform terms, and inflating follower counts could be misleading about popularity (general art. 6 UCPD).
- **JitPlus as tool provider:** if the product is designed to make reviews conditional or to hide incentives, JitPlus could be treated as having "commissioned" or facilitated the practice (complicity). Mitigations: content-neutral design, mandatory disclosure prompts, and no rating-based gating.

## Q8. EU: GDPR and DSA relevance for a SaaS that facilitates these campaigns

### Takeaway
GDPR applies if JitPlus serves EU merchants or customers. The merchant is likely the controller and JitPlus a processor (art. 28 DPA), unless JitPlus reuses the data. The DSA is of limited direct relevance: JitPlus does not host public reviews (Google does). If JitPlus hosts merchant-facing or public content, it is at most a hosting service with notice-and-action duties.
